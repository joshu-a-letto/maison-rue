A SMALL REMINDER
“The best outfit is the one that lets you get on with your day.”

There is no perfect wardrobe. There is only the one that works for you.

PRACTICAL, NOT PRECIOUS

A few useful starting points

01

The gentle closet reset

A wardrobe reset is not a challenge to get rid of half your clothes. It is a chance to reconnect with what you already have. Set aside an hour, put on something comfortable, and work in small sections rather than emptying every drawer at once.

Start with the pieces you wear on repeat. Ask what makes them easy: the fit, fabric, colour, or the fact that they work for a typical Tuesday. Group similar items together so you can spot duplicates and gaps. Put away out-of-season pieces, and set aside anything that needs a button, a hem, or a wash.

For the uncertain pile, try each piece on with something you already wear. If it still does not feel right, give yourself time to decide; a maybe box stored out of sight for a month can make the choice clearer. The goal is a wardrobe you can see and use, not a number of items to hit.

  • Keep a short note of the outfits you actually reach for this week.
  • Make a small repairs pile and handle one easy fix at a time.
  • Before buying anything, check whether you own something that already does the job.
02

Find your three-piece outfit formula

An outfit formula is a helpful starting point, not a uniform you have to follow. Try building around three parts: a comfortable base, a layer that suits the weather, and one small detail that makes the outfit feel like yours.

Your base might be straight-leg trousers and a tee, a simple dress, or a shirt with jeans. Add the layer you will genuinely need—an overshirt, cardigan, blazer, or coat. Finish with one choice you enjoy: a belt, a favourite pair of earrings, a colourful sock, or a bag that carries everything.

When a combination works, take a quick photo or write it down. After a few weeks you may notice a pattern: shapes you feel good in, colours that mix easily, and the layers your days call for. Use those clues when you shop your own wardrobe first.

  • Pick a base that fits the day, not an imagined version of your day.
  • Keep one reliable layer near the door for unpredictable weather.
  • Change just one part when an outfit feels off; it is often enough.
03

A plain-English guide to clothing care labels

Care symbols are small, but they can save a favourite piece from an avoidable laundry mishap. The tub symbol means washing; the number inside is the maximum temperature. A hand in the tub means hand wash, and a crossed-out tub means do not wash at home.

A triangle is about bleach: a plain triangle allows it, while a crossed-out one means skip it. The square covers drying. A circle inside it means tumble drying, and dots indicate heat; a crossed-out symbol means air-dry instead. An iron symbol shows ironing temperature, again with dots for heat. A crossed-out iron means do not iron.

When a label says dry clean, check the fabric and the maker's care notes before deciding. For everyday wear, airing clothes between uses, washing full loads on a cool setting when suitable, and treating stains early can all help. Always follow the specific label first—fabric blends and finishes can change what is safe.

  • Close zips and turn printed or delicate pieces inside out before washing.
  • Use a mesh bag for small or delicate items to reduce tangling.
  • When unsure, choose the gentlest care the label permits.
